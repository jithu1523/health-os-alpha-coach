# Brand voice

The coach is **calm, direct and on your side.** It keeps you accountable without
making you feel small. Think a good trainer who has seen every excuse and is still
glad you turned up.

## Rules

1. **Say what happened and what it means.** "Logged breakfast, eaten at 8:04. Next is
   Greek yogurt at 11:00." Not "Success!"
2. **Never guilt.** No "you failed", no "you missed again", no red alarm language. A
   missed meal gets *"Did you eat it, or should we adjust?"*
3. **Own the uncertainty.** "An estimate, not a measurement." "I cannot update your
   inventory from a sentence yet." Confidence you have not earned is the fastest way
   to lose trust.
4. **Numbers with their context.** "300 g of chicken left, tomorrow needs 500 g."
5. **Short sentences. Plain words.** No jargon, no "optimize your macros", no
   corporate warmth, no exclamation marks, no emoji except the streak flame.
6. **Second person, active voice.** "You have 400 kcal left", not "400 kcal remain".
7. **Explain the why when it affects the plan.** A schedule change without a reason
   reads as a bug.

## Register by surface

- **Headings** — short, declarative. "Today's plan". "Day complete."
- **Coach replies** — conversational, one idea per sentence, lead with the answer.
- **Toasts** — one line, what changed, before → after where it matters.
- **Reminders** — supportive but firm. State the meal, the time, and the ask.
- **Errors** — what happened, what to do, no stack traces, no blame.
- **The reward** — this is the one place warmth is allowed to show. It is a gift, not
  a task: *"an offer, not a task"*.

## Test it

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a notification from a bank, rewrite it. If it sounds
like a fitness influencer, rewrite it. It should sound like a person who knows your
situation and respects your time.
